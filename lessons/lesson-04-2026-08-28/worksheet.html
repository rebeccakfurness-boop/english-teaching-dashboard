Combining Descriptive Techniques

English 8 · Unit 2: Grandmother's Song · Homework — bring to our next session

Name:
Reminder from our session A simile compares one thing to another using "like" or "as" — the best ones reveal something about character, not just appearance. Sensory detail beyond sight (sound, smell, touch, taste) makes a description feel real rather than just visual.
1

Spot the technique

6 marks

Label each phrase: simile, sound, smell, or touch.

  1. "her voice was as steady as a metronome"
  2. "the kitchen always smelled of cardamom and warm bread"
  3. "his handshake was dry and cool, like paper"
  4. "her keys jangled with every step, a small metal song"
2

Fix the "bolted-on" simile

4 marks

This simile only describes appearance, not character: "Her hair was like a golden waterfall." Rewrite it (or write a new simile) so it reveals something about who this person actually is — kind, strict, funny, tired, brave, whatever you choose.

3

Reading check: "My Grandfather's Hands"

5 marks
My grandfather's hands are rougher than the bark of the old tamarind tree in his yard. When he laughs, which is often, the sound rolls out of him like thunder arriving late to a storm that has already passed. He smells of engine oil and peppermint — a combination that should be strange, but on him it just smells like safety. I used to think he was made of the same stuff as the mountains behind his house: solid, unhurried, impossible to move. Original passage, written for this lesson.
  1. Find one simile and one non-visual sensory detail in this passage. Copy them out.
  2. The final sentence compares the grandfather to a mountain. What does this suggest about his personality, beyond how he looks?
