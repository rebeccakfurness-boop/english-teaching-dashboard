English 8 · Unit 2 · Grandmother's Song

Combining Descriptive Techniques

Simile + sensory detail + two spellings to lock in for good

1 / 12

Warm-up

Describe someone — no sight allowed

Think of someone you know well. Describe them in one sentence — but you can't mention how they look.

2 / 12

Technique 1

Simile with purpose

❌ "Her hair was like a golden waterfall." (just looks)

✅ "Her laugh was like a door swinging open." (reveals character)
3 / 12

Technique 2

Sensory detail beyond sight

  • Sound — a laugh, footsteps, a particular phrase they always say
  • Smell — cooking, perfume, a workshop, the outdoors
  • Touch — a handshake, a hug, rough or soft hands
4 / 12

Combine them

Techniques working together

"He smells of engine oil and peppermint — a combination that should be strange, but on him it just smells like safety."
5 / 12

Bonus skill

Spelling strategy: chunk it

  • definitely — "finite" is hiding inside: de-FINITE-ly
  • necessary — one collar, two sleeves: 1 c, 2 s
  • believe — never believe a lie: i before e
  • resemble — re + semble, like "assemble"
6 / 12

Reading together

"My Grandfather's Hands"

Read it once for the picture it creates — then we'll go hunting for technique.

7 / 12

Text · Part 1

Touch & sound

My grandfather's hands are rougher than the bark of the old tamarind tree in his yard. When he laughs, which is often, the sound rolls out of him like thunder arriving late to a storm that has already passed.
8 / 12

Text · Part 2

Smell & the closing simile

He smells of engine oil and peppermint — a combination that should be strange, but on him it just smells like safety. I used to think he was made of the same stuff as the mountains behind his house: solid, unhurried, impossible to move.
9 / 12

Discuss

What does "mountain" tell us?

Not just size — think about character. What kind of person is "solid, unhurried, impossible to move"?

10 / 12

Your turn

  • Describe someone you know

  • 2 non-visual sensory details
  • Use 2 of today's spelling words correctly
