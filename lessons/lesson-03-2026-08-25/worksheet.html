3

Reading check: "The Last Ripe Mango"

6 marks
Grandmother held the mango up to the light and turned it slowly in her hand.

"You want the biggest one," she said. "That is not the same as wanting the best one."

"They're the same thing," I said. "Bigger is better."

She laughed — not unkindly — and set the mango back on the branch.

"Is it?" she asked. "Tell me that again in ten years."

I didn't understand her then. I only understood the mango.

"Can I have it now?" I asked.

"No," she said. "Not yet. Some things are worth the waiting." Original passage, written for this lesson.
  1. Find one place where a comma sits inside the closing quotation mark. Copy it out exactly.
  2. Grandmother mostly speaks in short sentences ("No," "Not yet.") while the child asks questions. What does this punctuation pattern suggest about each character?
  3. Why might the writer have used a dash for "— not unkindly —" instead of putting it in its own sentence?

One character should speak mostly in short, firm sentences; the other should ask questions or trail off with hesitation. Punctuate every line correctly.
