Verb Tenses for Effect

English 8 · Unit 1: Nature and humans · Homework for Friday 21 August

Name:

In our session, we looked at how writers choose between past simple, past continuous and past perfect to control how a story feels. This worksheet lets you practise spotting and using those choices on your own. Take your time — there's no strict word limit on Task 4, just aim for quality over quantity.

Reminder from our session Past simple = the main events, one after another. Past continuous = something already happening ("was/were + -ing") when another event interrupts it. Past perfect = something that happened even earlier, before the story's "now" ("had + past participle").
1

Label the tense

6 marks

Read each sentence. Underline the verb, then write which tense it is: past simple, past continuous, or past perfect.

  1. The wind had been growing stronger for hours before the first tree fell. → Tense:
  2. She was carrying the last box to the car when the power cut out. → Tense:
  3. The river burst its banks just after midnight. → Tense:
  4. By the time the rescue boats arrived, the water had already reached the rooftops. → Tense:
  5. Birds were circling above the valley all afternoon. → Tense:
  6. Nobody had warned the village that the dam was weak. → Tense:
2

Choose the tense that fits the effect

4 marks

For each pair, circle the sentence that better matches the effect described, then explain your choice in one sentence.

Effect wanted: a calm, ordinary moment about to be interrupted.
A) The old fisherman mended his nets on the beach.    B) The old fisherman was mending his nets on the beach.

Effect wanted: a fast, dramatic sequence of events.
A) The ground shook, the lights went out, and people ran for the doors.    B) The ground was shaking, the lights were going out, and people were running for the doors.

3

Reading check: "The Night the Mountain Woke"

5 marks

Re-read the passage from our session (below), then answer the questions.

For three hundred years, the volcano had slept. Farmers had planted their fields on its soft black soil; children had grown up believing the mountain was just a shape on the horizon, nothing more. Then, one warm evening in April, the ground began to hum.

At first, Mei thought it was a truck passing on the road below. She was washing dishes when the plates started to rattle in the sink. Somewhere outside, a dog was barking wildly, over and over, as if it already knew.

By midnight, the sky had turned the colour of rust. Ash fell like grey snow, and the whole village was moving — quietly, quickly — towards the coast road, carrying what they could and leaving everything else behind. Original passage, written for this lesson in the style of Unit 1's disaster narratives.
  1. Find one example of past perfect in paragraph 1. Why does the writer use it here, rather than past simple?
  2. Find one example of past continuous in paragraph 2. What is it interrupted by?
  3. Why do you think the writer switches to mostly past simple sentences in paragraph 3, as the action speeds up?
4

Your turn: write the opening of a disaster narrative

10 marks

Write 8–12 sentences describing the beginning of a natural event overwhelming a place (storm, flood, earthquake, wildfire — your choice). You must use all three tenses on purpose:

at least 1 past perfect sentence (backstory) at least 2 past continuous sentences (scene-setting / interrupted action) past simple for the main events

Before you start, jot a one-line plan: what backstory will your past perfect sentence give? What ordinary moment will your past continuous sentence interrupt?
