Verb Tenses for Effect

Choosing past simple, past continuous & past perfect on purpose

1 / 12

Warm-up

Which is more powerful?

  • A person, or a mountain?
  • A person, or the sea?
  • Name one natural event that feels bigger than any human.
2 / 12

Big idea

Writers pick tenses on purpose

Three "jobs" for the past:

  • Past simple — the main events, one after another
  • Past continuous — something already happening, then interrupted
  • Past perfect — something that happened even earlier
3 / 12

Tense 1

Past simple — the storyline

The ground shook. The lights went out. People ran.

One thing happens, then the next. It moves the story forward, fast.

4 / 12

Tense 2

Past continuous — mid-action

She was washing dishes when the plates started to rattle.

Something ordinary is already going on — then it gets interrupted. It slows things down and builds a scene.

5 / 12

Tense 3

Past perfect — the past of the past

For three hundred years, the volcano had slept.

This happened before the story's "now." It's how we give backstory without stopping the plot.

Reading together

"The Night the Mountain Woke"

Let's read it once for the story. Then we'll go back and hunt for all three tenses.

Text · Paragraph 1

Backstory first

For three hundred years, the volcano had slept. Farmers had planted their fields on its soft black soil; children had grown up believing the mountain was just a shape on the horizon. Then, one warm evening in April, the ground began to hum.
8 / 12

Text · Paragraph 2

An ordinary moment, interrupted

Mei thought it was a truck. She was washing dishes when the plates started to rattle. Somewhere outside, a dog was barking wildly, as if it already knew.
9 / 12

Text · Paragraph 3

Speeding up

By midnight, the sky had turned the colour of rust. Ash fell like grey snow, and the whole village was moving towards the coast road.
