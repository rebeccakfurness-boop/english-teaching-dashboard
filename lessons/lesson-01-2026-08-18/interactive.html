Reading · 4 min

"The Night the Mountain Woke"

An original passage written for this lesson, on the same theme as Unit 1's disaster narratives. Read it once for the story — then tap each "why?" card below.

Past Perfect Past Continuous Past Simple
For three hundred years, the volcano had slept. Farmers had planted their fields on its soft black soil; children had grown up believing the mountain was just a shape on the horizon, nothing more. Then, one warm evening in April, the ground began to hum.

At first, Mei thought it was a truck passing on the road below. She was washing dishes when the plates started to rattle in the sink. Somewhere outside, a dog was barking wildly, over and over, as if it already knew.

By midnight, the sky had turned the colour of rust. Ash fell like grey snow, and the whole village was moving — quietly, quickly — towards the coast road, carrying what they could and leaving everything else behind.
Sum it up

Three Tenses, Side by Side

TenseIts jobFeels likeExample
Past SimpleMoves the story forwardFast, urgent"The ground shook."
Past ContinuousSets a scene / gets interruptedSlow, tense"...was washing dishes when..."
Past PerfectGives backstoryDistant, explanatory"...had slept for 300 years."

Notice: none of these is "better" — they're tools. A strong writer reaches for whichever one does the job they need right now.
