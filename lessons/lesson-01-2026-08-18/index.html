Learning objective

By the end of this session, the student will be able to:

  • Explain, in his own words, what past simple, past continuous and past perfect each do in a story (not just their grammatical form).
  • Find examples of all three in a short passage and say why the writer chose each one.
  • Deliberately switch the tense of a sentence to change its effect — e.g. turning a flat statement into a suspenseful one.

Why this matters for the exam

Cambridge Lower Secondary English rewards writing that shows control of tense, not just accuracy. Markers specifically look for tense choices that create effect (tension, pace, flashback) rather than random switching or "tense drift" — where a student starts in the past simple and accidentally slides into the present. This lesson targets that skill directly.

Watch out for

Likely confusion point Students at this level often confuse past continuous ("was washing") with past simple + "-ing" as if it were a separate action word, rather than understanding it describes something already in progress when something else happened. The script below leans on a physical timeline gesture (drawing a line on paper, marking an X for the interrupting event) to make this concrete rather than abstract.
Likely confusion point Past perfect ("had slept") is the hardest of the three because it requires holding two points in the past in mind at once. Keep coming back to the phrase "the past of the past."
