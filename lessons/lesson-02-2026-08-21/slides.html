Poetic Form: Enjambment & Caesura

How line breaks and pauses control how a poem feels

1 / 11

Warm-up

Read it out loud

"The river climbed the bank and did not stop
until it reached the door."

  • Did your voice pause at the end of line 1, or keep going?
2 / 11

Technique 1

Enjambment

A line runs on into the next — no pause, no punctuation. The sentence isn't finished yet, so your eye is pulled forward.

"...and did not stop
until it reached the door."
3 / 11

Contrast

End-stopped lines

The opposite: the thought finishes exactly where the line ends. It feels complete, controlled, final.

"The sky was clear. Nobody thought to worry."
4 / 11

Technique 2

Caesura

A pause placed in the middle of a line — not the end. Usually shown by a comma, dash, semicolon or full stop.

"We counted days — one, two, three — before..."
5 / 11

Why it matters

Form creates effect

  • Enjambment → urgency, momentum, being swept forward
  • Caesura → a breath, a hesitation, emphasis on what comes right after
  • End-stopped lines → control, finality, closure
