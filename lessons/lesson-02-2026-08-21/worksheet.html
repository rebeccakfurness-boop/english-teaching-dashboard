Poetic Form: Enjambment & Caesura

English 8 · Unit 1: Nature and humans (closing) · Homework for Tuesday 25 August

Name:
Reminder from our session Enjambment = the sentence runs on past the end of the line, with no pause, pulling you forward. Caesura = a pause placed in the middle of a line, usually shown by punctuation (comma, dash, semicolon, full stop).
1

Enjambment or end-stopped?

4 marks

For each pair of lines, say whether the first line is enjambed (runs on) or end-stopped (the thought finishes at the line break).

  1. "The river climbed the bank and did not stop
    until it reached the door."
  2. "The sky was clear. Nobody thought to worry.
    By noon, the wind had changed."
  3. "We watched the water rise around the trees,
    the fence, the swing, the things we couldn't save."
2

Find the caesura

3 marks

Underline the punctuation mark that creates a caesura (a pause in the middle of the line) in each line below.

  1. We counted days — one, two, three — before the sky came back.
  2. Some things return. Some things do not.
  3. The dog barked once, then stopped, then said nothing more.
3

Mark up "Aftermath"

8 marks

Below is the poem from our session. Mark every enjambment with an arrow (→) at the end of the line, and every caesura with a double slash (//) at the pause. Then answer the two questions.

Aftermath

The mountain does not apologise.
It simply breathes out grey, and settles
across the roofs of houses no one
built for this kind of weather. We
counted days — one, two, three — before
the sky came back to something like blue,
and someone laughed, too loudly, at nothing,
just to hear a normal sound again.
Some things return. Some things do not.
The dog stopped barking eventually.
We never asked why. Original poem, written for this lesson.

a) Choose one enjambment you marked. What effect does it have on how you read that moment — does it speed you up, or pull you forward unexpectedly?

b) Choose one caesura you marked. Why might the poet have chosen to pause exactly there, rather than somewhere else in the line?

4

Your turn: write a short nature poem

10 marks

Write 6–10 lines about a natural event (real or imagined) affecting a place or a person. You must include:

at least 2 enjambed lines at least 2 caesuras 1 short, end-stopped line for impact

Underline your enjambments and mark your caesuras with // once you're done, exactly like you did in Task 3.

5

One-sentence exam answer

3 marks

Practise the sentence pattern examiners want to see: "The poet uses [technique] in the line '[quote]' to [effect]." Write one such sentence about your own poem from Task 4.
